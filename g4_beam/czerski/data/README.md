# Czerski v3 and Yuki 1997 primary data

## Provenance and interpretation

- Czerski et al., *Observation of Thermal Deuteron-Deuteron Fusion in Ion Tracks*, [arXiv:2409.02112v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02112v3), 28 September 2026, PDF SHA-256 `f72d91b34a0c9240bf280540bae6efa0b9e1ee2d691eb2494d512f0277c53c4b`. This is **v3**; the arXiv abstract metadata in some interfaces describes an earlier Zr-only version. Original numerical data are available from authors on request, paper p. 24. The files here contain plot digitizations, not the authors' raw data.
- Yuki et al., *J. Phys. G* **23**, 1459 (1997), [IAEA-NDS EXFOR O1084](https://github.com/IAEA-NDS/exfor_master/blob/2193cdb7066421fb7931b696d6bab219f9cc8b03/exforall/O10/O1084.x4), EXFOR revision 20191010. Subentry **O1084003** is titanium; O1084002 is ytterbium and is not included here.

`figure_points.csv` has one row per discernible experimental vector marker: figures 2a/b, 3a/b, 4a/b and 7a/b. Marker color/shape identifies D1+, D2+, D3+, except Fig. 3b does not distinguish species. Plot values, frame, target and species are explicit. A few overlapping path objects in 2a have exactly the same coordinates; exact duplicates are removed, but nearby points are retained. Measurements drawn again in different panels/figures are **not independent datasets**.

Figures 2a, 3a, 4a and 7a are proton yields, reported as the measured number of protons divided by particle current in microamperes and collection time in kiloseconds. This is detected counts/(microampere kilosecond) = 1000 detected counts/C; it is not the total number of emitted protons per coulomb without a detector acceptance/efficiency correction. 3a axis says deuteron energy, the main text and comparison with 3b establish lab energy per deuteron. For a stationary equal-mass target E_cm = E_lab/2; both columns are supplied. The magnet separates D1+, D2+, D3+; axes already express projectile energy **per deuteron**, so do not divide these energies by the molecular multiplicity again. Yield ordinates are per the deuteron particle current as defined by the paper, not per separately postulated 85/15 mixed beam.

2b, 4b and 7b are measured proton-peak **channel**, not yield. They cannot be converted to yield from a peak position alone: the paper's weighted-average model also needs a direct and a thermal/neutral spectral peak and their respective yields (Methods, Eqs. 24–25); it is not a second independent measurement of the corresponding yield. Fig. 3b is measured enhancement F=Y_exp/Y_scr(E,Ue=25 eV), not absolute yield. A reconstruction Y_exp=F*Y_scr is conditional on the chosen fitted screening curve and its normalization, correlated with Fig. 3a, and not an independent observed yield. Red squares in 3b do not identify beam species.

Experimental setup: saturated Zr, Ti0.993Pd0.007, Pd plates; Table 2 D/metal approximately 2, 2, 0.05. A 135-degree backward PIPS silicon detector measures the 2H(d,p)3H protons (other charged channels are also detected); shielding foil depends on target. 10 eV beam-energy definition and about 5 eV stability are authors' instrument statements, not digitization errors. Zr 2a caption reports statistical error bars and about 10% systematic uncertainty from target deuteron density. The figure markers have been digitized, **not the statistical bars**; blank/unspecified statistical uncertainties must not be interpreted as zero. Peak-position fit precision is approximately 0.2 channel for high-statistics runs, about 0.5 channel for low-statistics runs, per Methods; this is not a uniform per-point error. Error correlations, energy spread and exact numerical points are not provided by the figure. The three D2+ near-overlapping Zr points at similar energies are retained as drawn.

Magnet selection does not remove all fast neutrals: ion-source D1+ neutrals bypass the deceleration lenses and Fig. 7 Zr is dominated at low energy by approximately 20 keV lab neutrals, fraction about 3e-4; Fig. 2 Zr D2+ has a 10 keV-per-deuteron neutral component, also about 3e-4. D3+ at a 12 kV source has approximately 4 keV-per-deuteron neutrals, and the authors estimate their yield contribution almost two orders below the thermal-plateau interpretation. These are described beam-state dependent contributions, not corrections already subtracted from the plotted points. Pd did not use deceleration, so the paper excludes the above-nominal neutral mechanism there. Ti used deceleration; the paper identifies D3+ as the lowest-energy series, but it does not tabulate a point-specific Ti neutral fraction. The paper gives multiple approximate fractions in different passages; do not interpret 3e-4 as an exact pointwise measurement.

`exfor_o1084003_ti.csv` is the complete 19-point EXFOR Ti table, plotted and then digitized by the EXFOR compiler from Yuki Fig. 1. The ordinate is thick-target proton yield **per beam dose**, normalized to the yield at E_lab=6.45 keV (=3.225 keV CM), not to 6.5 keV CM. The values and stated compiler digitization resolutions are copied; energy_cm is calculated by /2. Blank `statistical_error_percent` means not supplied in the EXFOR table, not zero. The four supplied percentages include point statistics and the normalization-run statistics, hence their correlations are not specified. EXFOR gives common digitization errors 0.018 keV lab and 8% yield. Ti was gas preloaded; accumulated deuterons change the target population during bombardment. A 50+200 micrometre Si delta-E/E telescope was used and the compiler says values were corrected for the CM/lab detector solid-angle ratio. Authors' Ti screening value is 19 ± 12 eV statistical, with systematic ±12 eV (stopping) and +4 eV (deuteron number); don't substitute this number for the observable. The measured descending 1.283–3.122 keV CM interval is a no-observed-plateau control, not proof that its plateau is exactly zero at all energies.

## Reproduce figure point extraction

Install Poppler `pdftocairo`; acquire the exact PDF above outside the deliverable and run:

```
python3 extract_figures.py /path/to/2409.02112v3.pdf > figure_points.csv
```

The script converts source pages 5, 6, 12, 23 to SVG in a temporary directory, extracts marker centers directly from small filled colored vector paths (including transformed circular paths), removes explicitly located legend symbols, maps to axis ticks linearly or logarithmically, and emits original PDF-point positions plus results. The calibration constants in `PANELS` use text tick centers (x tick centers adjusted by approximately 0.32 PDF point for optical alignment). `x_pdf_pt` and `y_pdf_pt` allow reconstruction/recalibration. Figure labels and the finite marker size limit coordinate precision; results have not been benchmarked against author data, and the displayed decimals are not a claim of that many significant digits. Do not treat digitization uncertainty as the experimental statistical error. Points merged or obscured in the PDF, covered by curves, or clipped outside axes cannot be recovered. Per-point statistical error bars and their correlations were not extracted. No script synthesizes points or digitizes model curves.

`table2.csv` transcribes Table 2 (M is the fitted normalization in Eq. 23, not an independently calibrated deuteron density). Blank hot-track screening means not stated. The Ti 370 eV entry is an **inferred hot-track value required by the authors' explanation**, not the measured cold screening 25 ± 10 eV. Relative plateau is normalized to deuteron density and the Zr plateau, unlike the directly reported plateau column. `table1.csv` transcribes Table 1: the hydride compositions are 1.74, 1.93 and 0.054, not the rounded Table 2 ratios. The central temperatures use 0.1 ps and deposited energy 0.58 keV for 2 keV incident deuterons. These are calculated, not measured temperatures.

## Thermal spike: authors' model, not an empirical free floor

Paper Eqs. 5–9 and Methods Eqs. 14–23 describe a proposed process, not a directly measured track temperature or established causal identification. With uniform local density N0 and hopping density N:

```
N = N0 exp[-EA/(kB T)]                                         (7)
R(T) = N0 N <sigma v>                                         (8)
<sigma v> = sqrt(8/pi)/(sqrt(mu)*(kB T)^(3/2))
            * integral_0^infinity sigma(E) E exp[-E/(kB T)] dE
R_narrow(T) = N0 N * [8 pi^(3/2) hbar^3 sqrt(ER) /
             (mu^2 a (kB T)^(3/2))] * sqrt(EG/Ue)
             * exp[-sqrt(EG/Ue)] * exp[-ER/(kB T)] * Gamma_p/Gamma
count_rate = (I_charge/e) * V * tau * (Omega/4pi) * R(T)       (9)
```

The paper writes I for the number of tracks/s; the explicit `I_charge/e` is for singly charged incident atomic deuterons. Molecular beams require actual track/deuteron flux accounting. R is reactions/(volume time), V is track volume, tau its lifetime; the estimate assumes uniform mean T, density and track properties, independent tracks and the stated detector solid angle. For lattice-bound D the paper explicitly uses N0*N rather than N²/2 for an all-mobile plasma: N is the small hopping population and N0 the trapped target density. E, ER, Ue, EG and kBT must be in one energy unit; mu, hbar and a must use consistent physical units. EG=986 keV; DD reduced mass mu and channel radius a=7 fm. The quoted narrow-resonance expression is conditional on the authors' approximation; width <1 eV alone does not guarantee it is narrow compared with kBT. At T=1080 K, kBT is about 0.093 eV, while the paper discusses total width about 0.2–0.3 eV: the approximation deserves care. The paper quotes proton width 40 meV and a 115-degree phase for Ti higher-energy resonance analysis, but neither a separate definite positive ER value nor a fitted total-width/branching value is supplied in the Table 2 data; the thermal absolute rate cannot be inferred uniquely by silently selecting them.

EA is 413 meV (Zr), 680 meV (Ti), 150 meV (Pd), from the cited diffusion references listed in Table 2. D/M is not density in cm^-3: N0 also requires material atomic density. As a rough **derived** estimate using the Table 1 hydride mass densities and stoichiometries, standard atomic weights, and N0 = rho*N_A*x/(A_metal+2.0141*x), N0 is about 6.21e22 (Zr), 8.92e22 (Ti), 3.67e21 (Pd) cm^-3. This assumes the listed density describes the stated hydride composition and homogeneous stoichiometry; it is not a direct density measurement and differs from using rounded Table 2 D/M ratios. Thermal density enters as N0² exp[-EA/(kBT)], not N0 exp[-EA/(kBT)] alone.

The authors estimate mean track T=1080 K, V=1870 nm³ for Zr, tau=100 ps and Omega/(4pi)=0.0032, using 90 microampere at 2 keV lab as an example; they predict about 1 detected proton/100000 s, approximately three times below their observed rate. A spherical heat-diffusion estimate yields central temperatures of Table 1, and the text discusses 100–1000 ps lifetimes, recrystallization and roughly 10% electronic-to-lattice energy transfer. These are physical-model assumptions, not fit-free established inputs for other materials or beam energies. They quote 1–2 orders of magnitude absolute-rate uncertainty. The Ti hypothetical hot screening of 370 eV is especially consequential because the rate contains exp[-sqrt(EG/Ue)]; cold 25 eV cannot be silently replaced with the inferred hot value.

For direct yield the paper defines Y_scr=N0 integral sigma_scr dx, electronic stopping |dE/dx|=C sqrt(E), and its approximate fit Y_scr(E_cm)=M exp[-sqrt(EG/(E_cm+Ue))]. The M values in Table 2 are fit coefficients in that approximation; they are not a universal absolute yield calibration. The published 57 keV barn broad S-factor and Ti threshold-resonance/interference are described in Methods Eqs. 14–17. Combining direct and thermal contributions means converting R through track volume/lifetime and detector normalization; inserting a free fitted additive floor is not the paper's physical thermal-rate model.
