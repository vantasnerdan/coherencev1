# Reaction source grounding

## Decision and observable

The reaction question is whether a **single intrinsic energy-dependent D–D strength**, integrated through each foil's already-saved, energy-specific transport profile and stopping, can account for the four published shapes with 40 eV baseline screening. If not, a reaction explanation needs a **quantified material/loading-dependent entrance response** at fixed profile, or an explicitly modeled host-dependent decay/branching effect; neither is established by merely postulating a common resonance. Do not fit D inventory, depth, site fractions, sheet amplitude or transport. A common local-energy multiplier does **not** generally cancel between thick-target materials: their fixed kernels weight local collision energies differently. It cancels for an energy-independent multiplier, a common multiplier applied at the same nominal-energy observable boundary, or identical normalized kernels. The cross-foil decision requires the actual response-weighted integrals.

For material `m`, write `F_m(E) = [Y_m/C]_candidate(E) / [Y_m/C]_40eV,smooth(E)` for the physical same-energy factor, separately from the data-shape comparison `R_m(E) = [Y_m/C](E) / [Y_m/C](6.5 keV CM)`. The latter equals 1 by construction at its own reference; `F_m(6.5)` is not forced to 1. Required nominal CM energies are **0.25, 0.5, 1, 2.5, 6.5 keV**. Do not turn a reaction modification into an apparent screening energy.

[Karahadian et al., Nat. Commun. 17, 8845 (2026), Fig. 2 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74421-1) report proton and neutron counts per delivered Coulomb, normalized to each foil's own 6.5-keV-CM reference. The proton channel establishes the lowest-energy floor; neutrons cover the higher-energy overlapping region, not the lowest points. Methods describe channel-efficiency correction and combination assuming near-equal p/n branching, with proton spectra from 0.25–4.5 keV CM and neutron spectra from 4.5–7 keV CM; a p/n stitch is consequently a measurement-model assumption, not a direct sub-keV neutron observation. Compare the channel-specific yields before assuming the common stitch. Published [SI Table 1](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-026-74421-1/MediaObjects/41467_2026_74421_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) supplies signal/background rates and current, not raw run likelihoods; the prior `g7_gap/README.md` §4 reports a Fig. 2/SI shape discrepancy of 4.68–4.89 at 0.25 versus 3 keV and alternative current-normalization hypotheses, **not** proof of a double correction. Do not reconstruct or invent raw counts.

## Threshold resonance: source-native equations and assumptions

[Czerski, Phys. Rev. C 106, L011601 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevC.106.L011601), full open paper, proposes a near-DD-threshold ⁴He `0+` state and interference with the broad `0+` amplitude. This is an author interpretation of keV proton data and a nuclear-cluster model, not an independently measured resonance location or a proven host mechanism. The metallic Zr three-parameter fit gives proton partial width `Γp = 40 ± 15 meV`, phase `116 ± 40°`, and screening parameter `109 ± 30 eV`; fixing phase to 110° gives `40 ± 10 meV`. The gas-target fit gives `10 ± 2 meV`; the author's explanation in terms of thermal/solid broadening and suggestion of an intrinsic width below 1 meV are speculative, not measured intrinsic widths.

[Czerski et al., arXiv:2409.02112v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02112v3), Methods Eqs. 14 and 17, uses, in the source's notation and conventions,

`S(E) = S_B + S_0(E) + 2 sqrt(S_B S_0(E)/3) cos φ_B`,

`S_0(E) = sqrt(2 E_G) ħ³ Γp / (a μ^(3/2) E²)`.

Here `S_B = 57 keV b` in that paper, `E_G ≈ 986 keV`, `a = 7 fm`, and `μ` is DD reduced mass. The factor `1/3` belongs **inside** the interference square root: only the indicated broad `0+` component interferes, not all of `S_B`. At fixed `E`, `a`, `μ`, the resonant **cross-section/S term is linear in Γp**, its amplitude and interference scale as `sqrt(Γp)`, and the sign follows `cos φ_B`; a change to Γp is not a direct linear change to the full coherent signal. The project's common-proton model retains the source-literal Eq. 17 with `Γp=40 meV`, `φ=115°`, `a=7 fm`, and its smooth Bosch–Hale `S_B(E)` rather than the source's constant 57 keV b. `g4_beam/forward/README.md` flags that substitution of the printed Eqs. 15–16 into the printed cross-section convention appears to produce an extra π relative to Eq. 17; do not hide this source-convention discrepancy by silently changing Γp. Neutron width and phase are not established by the proton model; a mirrored neutron term is a separately labeled sensitivity, not data.

The general source Breit–Wigner term is proportional to `Γd(E) Γp / [(E−E_R)² + Γtot(E)²/4]`; the resonant amplitude is proportional to `sqrt(Γd Γp)/(E−E_R + iΓtot/2)`, up to its phase and normalization. `Γtot = Γd + Γp + Γn + Γem` (including IPC); `Γp` alone is **not** the total width. In the keV beam reduction used for Eqs. 14/17, `E_R → 0` and the denominator is approximated by `E²`; the width is therefore not an independently resolved keV-fit parameter. The entrance width depends on penetration and on the assumed single-particle reduced width; thermal-rate formulas instead retain the proton branching fraction `Γp/Γtot` and the Boltzmann factor `exp(−E_R/kT)`. A host shift of an eV-scale near-threshold pole can matter even though an eV is negligible next to MeV exit energies. Do not apply `E_R→0` simultaneously to a thermal track where its value controls this factor.

The [2020 Czerski et al. paper, Acta Phys. Pol. B 51, 649](https://doi.org/10.5506/APhysPolB.51.649), §3, explicitly **assumes** `E_R=1 eV` and `Γtot=0.5 eV` for thermal extrapolation; its accelerator data do not uniquely determine the pole. The 2022 paper's illustrative Fig. 3 instead assumes `Γtot=0.1 eV` and 1 or 10 eV; 2024 v3 discusses a total width around 200–300 meV. These are different source conventions/estimates, not one measured universal width. The 2020 source cautions that a constant accelerator-inferred screening shift is inaccurate when screening is no longer small compared with collision energy; its cited potential model approaches about 78% at ~1 eV. The 2024 thermal narrow-resonance/Maxwell model assumes a narrow width, hot diffusion, screening, and effective track volume and lifetime; its authors quote a 1–2 order-of-magnitude absolute estimate uncertainty. The reported Zr, Ti and Pd plateau spectra and fitted parameters do not themselves establish this thermal mechanism or transfer its hot-screening assumptions to UC Davis.

## Branching and scale of a possible host effect

[Dubey et al., Phys. Rev. X 15, 041004 (2025), published arXiv v2, Table I and §III.D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07567v2) report detector-corrected **thick-target** IPC/proton yield ratios `0.42 ± 0.05` at 10 keV deuteron lab and `0.060 ± 0.012` at 20 keV lab (stationary-D nominal CM 5 and 10 keV), with statistical and systematic uncertainties; their 10/20 ratio is `7.0 ± 1.6`. These are not the fitted resonance partial-width parameter `ΓIPC/Γp = 14`, obtained with a small broad IPC component and destructive interference, under a particular fit (`Γp=20 meV`, IPC phase 170°, `S_IPC=0.25 keV b`). The paper also discusses 40 meV and a different proton phase in another calculation. Its Si and photon inferences depend on response/background and Geant4; p/n equality is an assumption over that measured region. There is no direct UC Davis sub-keV IPC branching measurement in these sources. `g7_gap/experiment_rows_addendum.csv` rows `dubey_ipc_10/20` preserve these semantics.

**Decision-scale estimate, not a bound on all nuclear mechanisms.** The open exits have `Q_p ≈ 4.03 MeV`, `Q_n ≈ 3.27 MeV`, and IPC pair kinetic release ≈22.82 MeV. An ordinary smooth 1 eV host energy change is only about `2–3×10⁻⁷` of p/n Q and `4×10⁻⁸` of the IPC release; even 100 eV is only a few `10⁻⁵` of the p/n scale. Thus, absent a nearby exit-channel threshold, sharp structure, cancellation or large electronic coupling, smooth final-state phase-space/Coulomb or IPC-openness changes are naturally tiny, not order-one branch changes. This does **not** rule out an electronic influence on an eV-scale threshold pole, entrance penetrability, interference near a cancellation, or an explicitly calculated environment-dependent width. A 1 eV shift compared with a 1 eV pole displacement or sub-eV width can be order one; its effect depends on detuning and on whether shifts are common to the entrance threshold and pole, not merely on their absolute values. Material-dependent initial-state screening/penetration can be exponentially sensitive at low energy without changing D density; quantitatively link it to the same nuclear amplitude and the fixed-profile source weighting rather than declaring final-state widths tunable.

Prior `g7_gap/README.md` §Decision reports a **conditional** frozen-background total-DD multiplier 5.02 at 0.5 keV lab, not a new foil yield calculation. Its §4 reports that, in a prior common-proton uniform-target comparison, the fixed nuclear model changed fitted parameters by less than 10 eV under one current-normalization convention; this does not establish a general bound for all fixed transported profiles. The unresolved decision is whether a response-weighted common resonance meets all four measured shapes and p/n/IPC constraints, and, if not, what sized material-specific entrance or decay effect supplies the remaining difference.
