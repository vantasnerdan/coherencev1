# Material evidence for the PdO/Pd beam comparison

## Decision anchors

- The directly relevant Kasagi observable is the **relative D(d,p)T proton yield**, normalized to bracketing 10-keV measurements on each prepared foil. The reported 600 ± 20(stat) ± 75(sys) eV for PdO and 310 ± 20 ± 50 eV for Pd are *fitted effective shifts*, not direct voltage or local-density measurements. A changed D profile, stopping, temperature or incident energy is a competing interpretation, not a demonstrated explanation of this particular difference. The experiment used different prepared targets. [K, H]
- An approximately 30 nm PdO layer was measured by SIMS. **No source examined here establishes D accumulation underneath that oxide during the Kasagi experiment**, an oxide-interface D/Pd value, its through-film resistivity under irradiation, or a PdO breakdown field. An uptake barrier is not by itself evidence of subsurface accumulation: it can inhibit entry as well as exit. [K, O, N]
- The current correction is **direct suppressed-cup current / target current = 0.97 ± 0.03** for 3–10 keV across the investigated materials; it is not a resolved PdO secondary-electron yield or an oxide-trapping fraction. Positive ion impact and escaping electrons both charge a floating surface positively, which tends to **retard**, not accelerate, subsequent D+ ions. The measured target current already contains its emission contribution; it must not be added again. [K]
- Independent PdO films can be conducting: the reported in-plane DC conductivity of particular thermally oxidized films at 40 °C is approximately 4–11 S/cm, sample-dependent. Applying it to a 30-nm irradiated PdO/Pd contact would be an unsupported extrapolation, but assuming a perfect insulator would be equally unsupported. See the explicitly conditional electrical estimates below. [O]

## Density, depth profile and available inventory

**What the original figure means.** Kasagi's Sec. 2 explicitly says that the absolute density abscissa of Fig. 2 was obtained using a D(d,p)T cross section and an assumed constant target-D density with depth. Fig. 1 uses the same proton-yield-based density method. Rough visual readings, not digitized observations or uncertainty intervals, from Fig. 1 at 10 keV are:

| Prepared foil | Approximate plotted saturation-density range, D/cm³ | Temperature span |
|---|---:|---:|
| PdO/Pd/Au | ~3 × 10²⁰ to ~1.5 × 10²¹ | ~230 to ~170 K |
| Pd metal | ~1 × 10²¹ to ~6 × 10²¹ | ~230 to ~170 K |

Fig. 2's 10-keV monitor distributions visually center near ~1.2 × 10²¹ D/cm³ for PdO and ~3.8 × 10²¹ D/cm³ for Pd. These are **yield-equivalent uniform-model densities**, not measurements of the D concentration at an oxide surface, under the interface, throughout the metal, or as a function of depth. They also are not universal lower and upper physical bounds. Fig. 2 gives 86 PdO and 48 Pd monitors and quotes 12% dispersion for PdO; that repeatability does not validate a uniform profile. Foil surface temperature was thermocouple-monitored, liquid-nitrogen cooled; its microscopic beam-spot temperature need not coincide with the reading. [K]

For a common *metal-equivalent comparison only*, Pd atom density ~6.8 × 10²² cm⁻³ gives Fig. 1 Pd metal values of D/Pd ~0.015–0.09 and the PdO-foil yield-equivalent values ~0.004–0.022. The latter divisor does **not** locate the reactions in Pd metal or determine PdO stoichiometry. If instead one assumes uniform D in crystalline PdO, its tetragonal cell parameters a = 0.3043 nm, c = 0.5336 nm and two Pd per cell give ~4.05 × 10²² Pd/cm³; the same PdO-foil Fig. 1 numbers would correspond to ~0.007–0.037 D per Pd in that *different, hypothetical* host. A spatial mixture cannot be identified from these conversions. [K, B]

For an upper inventory scale, fcc Pd has one octahedral interstitial site per Pd, so D/Pd ≈ 1 is a useful **single-occupancy site-count benchmark**, not a theorem forbidding additional D at surfaces, defects, tetrahedral sites, bubbles or nonequilibrium high-pressure states. The independently prepared palladium in Chen et al. was electrochemically loaded to approximately D/Pd = 0.7 by separate in-situ XRD; the authors explicitly say the ratio was **not directly measured during the beam experiment**, and seek future near-surface characterization. It does not measure a PdO/Pd interface. The authors quote ~1 at room temperature as their own capacity context, not a universal bound for an irradiated heterogeneous interface. [C]

By contrast, a genuine depth-resolved comparator exists for another surface: Namba et al. used calibrated ¹H(¹⁵N,αγ)¹²C NRA on clean Pd(110) and 0.3 monolayer Au/Pd(110) after 1,000 L H₂ at 120 K. At 5 nm they report 4.2 and 13 atomic percent H, respectively (if the denominator is total Pd+H, H/Pd ≈ 0.044 and 0.15). This is hydrogen, not deuterium; Au/Pd is not PdO/Pd. Their NRA profiles establish that some prepared *metal* surfaces can have depth-dependent uptake, not that oxide traps D beneath it. [N]

Didyk et al. report ERDA/RBS after 25-keV D implantation into Pd near 30 °C, at 1.2–2.3 × 10²² D/m², with analysis after 10 days and three months; they describe Pd D concentrations of less than ~1–2% near their ERDA detection limit. This is a delayed, higher-energy bare-metal comparison, not a lower bound on in-beam loading. Their text also contains inconsistent qualitative retention statements; do not promote it into an in-situ PdO-interface estimate. [D]

## Oxide, stopping and reaction-weighted depth

Kasagi prepared 40–50 µm rolled Pd, oxygen-flame annealed around 1000 °C, and electrochemically deposited Au on one side; SIMS established ~30 nm PdO. The source does not give a D depth profile or a stopping distribution in this oxide. Kasagi states effective depths contributing to DD reactions in **Pd metal** of ~5 nm at 2.5 keV and ~15 nm at 10 keV, and says >70% of the 10-keV yield originates within the first 5 nm. These are **reaction-yield weighting statements, not projected ion ranges**; neither can be transplanted to PdO. [K]

A distinct stopping proxy: Didyk et al. Table 1 gives SRIM-2008 for **25-keV D in Pd metal**, projected range 1237 ± 458 Å, i.e. 124 ± 46 nm (the second number is range straggling), at Pd atomic density 6.803 × 10²² cm⁻³. Chen et al. report a modeled ~0.18 µm penetration depth for 30-keV D in Pd. These are higher-energy metal calculations, not experimental PdO stopping curves or direct 2.5–10-keV ranges. [D, C]

As an order-of-magnitude *conditional extrapolation only*, if Pd electronic stopping S(E) ∝ √E held all the way from 25 keV to zero and its coefficient were unchanged, the CSDA path integral R(E)=∫dE/S(E) would scale as √E. Scaling 124 nm at 25 keV would give ~39 nm at 2.5 keV and ~78 nm at 10 keV in Pd. The source's 124 nm is a projected SRIM range, however, not necessarily the CSDA path length; nuclear stopping, straggling, composition and the PdO/Pd interface make this an illustrative magnitude rather than a prediction of transmission or reaction depth in the real oxide. Kasagi's actual comparison used the Bosch–Hale cross section and Andersen–Ziegler stopping, with its own stated stopping and depth-profile systematics. [K, D]

## Beam current, signs, charging and DC conduction

The 4-mm-diameter beam footprint has area A = π(2 mm)² ≈ 1.26 × 10⁻⁵ m². Kasagi reports 60 µA at 10 keV for conditioning and I ∝ 1/E to maintain input power in the measurement. **If** the 10-keV measurement monitor also used 60 µA, then measurement current would run 240 → 60 µA from 2.5 → 10 keV and footprint-averaged current density 19.1 → 4.77 A/m², assuming a uniformly filled circular spot. The monitor current itself is not separately specified; local peak current density could differ. Keeping total input power fixed is not keeping local surface power density fixed; the authors explicitly note that distinction. [K]

Define Jᵢ as true incident singly charged ion current density, δₑ as *net escaping* electrons per incident ion and Jₜ as the positive target-current magnitude. In the idealized case with no other currents, Jₜ = Jᵢ(1 + δₑ). Interpreting the central 0.97 ratio under precisely that model gives δₑ ≈ 1/0.97 − 1 ≈ 0.031; the ratio's stated ±0.03 and unreported sample-specific effects mean this is **not** a measured intrinsic δₑ for PdO or a validated oxide charging current. Secondary electrons that return, electrical contacts, backscattered charged ions, leakage and neutralization change the accounting. In particular, do not take the already measured Jₜ and add δₑJᵢ a second time. Electron-induced SEY cannot be substituted for D+-induced yield: Gao et al.'s partially oxidized Ti/Pd films gave δmax = 0.87 at 400 eV **incident electrons**, not deuterons; their samples and beam do not establish the PdO value here. Deuteron-induced emission measurements do exist for clean titanium deuteride, but the accessible abstract reports 80–254 keV rather than this 2.5–10-keV PdO condition. [K, G, T]

For an illustrative planar dielectric of thickness L = 30 nm and relative permittivity εᵣ (not measured for the actual film), C/A = ε₀εᵣ/L. A 300 V surface-to-metal magnitude would require E = 1 × 10¹⁰ V/m and |Q|/A = 0.0885 εᵣ C/m² = 5.53 × 10¹⁷ εᵣ elementary charges/m². At εᵣ = 10 this is ~0.89 C/m² or ~5.5 × 10¹⁸ charges/m². Against the Pd atoms in 5 nm, nPd × 5 nm ≈ 3.4 × 10²⁰/m², it is ~1.6% of one elementary charge per Pd atom; this is a capacitor comparison, **not** a prediction that this much D is ionized or retained. If *all* the above assumed incident current continuously charged this capacitor with no return or leakage, the corresponding 300 V charging times would be ~0.046–0.19 s. No measured trapping fraction or persistent voltage follows; positive surface charge opposes D+ entry, and neutralization/metallic screening and contact geometry matter.

Likewise, a homogeneous, ohmic, through-thickness DC model would give |V| = Jₗₑₐₖ ρ L. To sustain 300 V at Jₗₑₐₖ = 4.77–19.1 A/m² requires ρ ≈ 2.1 × 10⁹–5.2 × 10⁸ Ω m (≈2.1 × 10¹¹–5.2 × 10¹⁰ Ω cm). If the actual leakage is a fraction f of the incident current, required ρ increases by 1/f; actual f is unknown. This is **DC electrical stress**, not a measured breakdown threshold or proof of breakdown. A positive surface potential would also modify beam energy rather than demonstrate a microscopic screening potential.

For the delivery owner's particular conditional **−650.6 V** surface-to-metal scenario, the same 30 nm gives |E| = 2.169 × 10¹⁰ V/m = 21.69 GV/m. With the same assumed 4.77–19.1 A/m² entirely balanced by through-film leakage, |ρ| = |V|/(JL) ≈ 4.54 × 10⁹–1.14 × 10⁹ Ω m; for a smaller leakage fraction it must be proportionately larger. This is a model requirement, not a measured PdO property or breakdown comparison. The sign is essential: positive charging by D+ arrival and escaping electrons does not itself create the assumed negative surface potential. At εᵣ = 10 the planar charge magnitude would be ~1.92 C/m² (~1.20 × 10¹⁹ elementary charges/m²), not a measured trapped charge.

For a real **ion-induced** electron-yield comparator, Ke et al. report a maximum ~1.37 electrons per deuteron at 143 keV on self-sputter-cleaned titanium deuteride, within 80–254 keV; this is neither the PdO material nor the present energy regime and cannot supply its δ. [T]

Ozabaci et al. sputtered initially 60-nm Pd on quartz then oxidized in dry air for four hours at 550, 650 or 750 °C; they measured lateral DC I–V with two Ag contacts, 5-mm separation, under dry air. Their Fig. 7(e) plots approximately 4–11 S/cm at 40 °C and 7–14 S/cm at 200 °C across these three films (rough graph readings), i.e. ρ ~0.09–0.25 Ω cm at 40 °C; their reported activation energies are 44.8, 92.1 and 52.7 meV. **The methods text says room temperature to 160 °C while Fig. 7 labels 40–200 °C**; the figures are cited as plotted rather than resolving that inconsistency. Values are in-plane, DC, porous/polycrystalline, sample- and environment-dependent, not bulk intrinsic PdO and not through-plane data for Kasagi's 170–230 K, irradiated PdO/Pd contact. Hydrogen exposure can alter the phase: this study reports room-temperature conversion of PdO-650 to Pd after >2 h at 2% H₂, whereas smaller-concentration response is reversible; other conditions could behave differently. [O]

Ryabtsev et al. deposited initial 30/90-nm Pd films and oxidized them in air; TEM shows that heating conditions can fragment the 30-nm film, with loss of lateral conductivity at sufficiently severe annealing. This does not show fragmentation in Kasagi's foil, but illustrates why film morphology and contact path cannot be assumed. No primary measurement examined here gives the applicable PdO DC breakdown field, irradiated through-plane resistivity, relative permittivity at the relevant frequency, or δₑ for 2.5–10-keV D+ on this PdO. ZrO₂ is at most a *named, different dielectric comparator*; no ZrO₂ breakdown or conductivity substitutes for the missing PdO data. [R]

## Competing interpretations and scope

Huke et al. formulate the thick-target observable as Y(E)=∫₀ᴿ n_D(x) σ(E(x)) dx and emphasize that density-profile changes, surface contamination, stopping and the cross section enter together; their critique of published interpretations is not a measurement proving what happened to this particular oxide foil. In Kasagi, 10-keV bracketing reduces some temporal drift but does not identify a depth profile or guarantee identical local temperatures. The paper's statement that its own estimate should be treated as a lower limit is its conditional thermal argument, not a model-independent bound in the face of other profile alternatives. [K, H]

The retained project result is ~38–39 eV static screening at 0.1 Å for sampled Pd/interface configurations, with a 40-eV energy-independent shift used as a comparison model; the project README correctly says the sampled short-range static lowering does not establish a constant barrier shift throughout the turning region. This note does not perform a refit, scientific computation, new DFT, or validation, and does not establish a mechanism for the 600–310 eV fitted difference. [P]
