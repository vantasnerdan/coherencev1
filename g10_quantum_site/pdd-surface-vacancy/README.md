# G10 — Deuteron wells at the covered PdD(111) face and in a Pd vacancy

`surface-reference.json` is the relaxed 2×2 PdD(111) slab: one adsorbed deuteron per four top-plane Pd atoms (0.25 ML), original D28 above the top face, four bottom Pd held fixed. The maximum mobile force was 0.04838 eV/Å against the 0.05 eV/Å criterion. Original G9 receiving D26 moves `(0.03606, 0.02080, −0.54880)` Å from the ideal location; the norm is 0.55038 Å. This compares a relaxed covered slab to the G9 ideal geometry. It does not isolate coverage from lattice relaxation and is not a trajectory or a quantum width.

`site-wells.json` is the replayed consumer output. Each site was moved alone by ±0.12 and ±0.36 Å along three fixed axes; every other ion and cell was held. Positions and full Cartesian forces retain the original zero-based atom order. D28 and D26 use the same relaxed geometry and surface zero. The outward normal is positive; the two fixed in-plane unit vectors are recorded in `ray_units`. Projected forces are along the fixed axis regardless of the sign of the displacement. Compare relative electronic free energies only against the zero of the same geometry and setting.

The vacancy uses the inherited G7 C geometry and original D7. Its `normal` points from the cavity center to the as-given D; the origin is the D, not the center. Moving a further +0.36 Å lowers the energy by 0.03403 eV and the force still points outward. The two transverse directions rise in energy for both sampled signs. This is a fixed-neighbor cut from a nonstationary reference, not evidence of a stable vacancy-centered trap. Do not turn that ray into a bound distribution without first establishing confinement.

In the slab, D26's normal displacement costs about 0.08 eV at ±0.36 Å, versus about 0.20 eV for D28; both repel displacement along the measured normal cut. Those are sampled, static wells, not an occupation model and not evidence for impact-time screening. The underlying model is finite-cell, neutral Born–Oppenheimer PBE/PAW; it omits electron–phonon damping, real-time nuclear motion, and the changing potential during ion arrival.

`points/` contains the exact native records. `grid_comparison` in the export compares same-geometry zero and one displacement at each FD grid: adsorbed `normal-p12` on the surface and `in1-p12` in the vacancy. These checks address those two sampled contrasts, not all directions or the entire well. Atom geometry does not change in either grid check. `SHA256SUMS` binds the archive members. Replay from this directory with `python replay/readout_site.py points <output file>`.
