# Declared single-site model (before numerical implementation)

The measured inputs here are frozen-ion electronic free-energy rises and forces of the constrained, fully occupied cubic fluorite calculation, not experimental Ti foil spectra. Position is relative to source atom 8, in conventional cubic Cartesian coordinates. +[111] moves away from its single nearest Ti toward the other three; −[111] moves toward that Ti. These sides are never averaged as equivalent. [100]/[110] do have source twofold rotations; their very small signed residuals will be reported before symmetry averaging. Slopes are −force projected on the signed displacement coordinate.

Let t=x²+y²+z², S2=x²y²+y²z²+z²x² and S3=x²y²z². The chosen tetrahedral interpolation is

V(x,y,z)=t a(t)+4 S2 b(t)+27 S3 c(t)+3√3 xyz d(t).

At positive r the four coefficients are a=E100/r², b=(E110−E100)/r⁴, c=[(E111+ + E111−)/2−E100−4(E110−E100)/3]/r⁶ and d=(E111+−E111−)/(2r³). Coefficient values AND derivatives with respect to t are derived from the four measured energies and radial slopes at r=0.12,0.24,0.48,0.80 Å. Cubic Hermite interpolation in t preserves each anchor and its slope. Below the first anchor, each coefficient continues as its first-node tangent in t. Thus the origin is a genuine Cartesian polynomial, with common quadratic curvature, an allowed xyz odd tetrahedral term, and no angular cusp from dividing independent ray interpolants by r. Interpolation between anchors and the otherwise unidentified angular surface remain modeling assumptions, not measured 3D data.

For r>0.8 Å, the angular surface and radial slope at 0.8 Å are continued with a positive isotropic quadratic rise (curvature 4 eV/Å²). This is a conditional localized single well, not a periodic diffusion calculation or a claim that the octahedral/interstitial region has no other minimum. The matched exterior is synthetic; any probability sampling it is labeled. The oscillator basis lives on all of R³ and introduces no reflecting hard wall. Exact diagonalization means eigenstates of this declared finite projected Hamiltonian, with selected basis/quadrature/observable differences reported, not an exact measured 3D potential.

A single inexpensive closure comparison adds a smooth nonnegative tetrahedral-invariant term, zero with zero radial derivative on all supplied rays: proportional to (x²−y²)²(y²−z²)²(z²−x²)²/(t+0.16 Å²)^4, calibrated to 0.05 eV at r=0.4 Å in direction (1,2,3). The same comparison stiffens the synthetic exterior to 8 eV/Å². It asks whether unmeasured angular/exterior support changes the isotope-tail interpretation, rather than treating a few rays as a uniquely identified surface. No potential refit or new electronic calculation is performed.

The retained Hamiltonian is H=−ħ²∇²/(2m)+V with H/D/T masses 1.00782503223, 2.01410177812 and 3.01604928199 u, respectively. These follow the existing G10 neutral-atom isotope-mass convention as an approximation to nuclear masses (the neglected electron changes H mass by 0.055%, less for D/T). Canonical eigenstate weights define all position and Fourier momentum densities; p=ħk. Real eigenstates guarantee even momentum occupation despite odd position structure. T_d symmetry makes the canonical Cartesian second moments equal; signed directional tails and plane marginals can still differ. No PdD coupling is transferred. Only the separately supplied frozen longitudinal nearest-pair line is used by the pair calculation.
