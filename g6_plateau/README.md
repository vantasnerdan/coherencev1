# Four plateau causes: forecasts before the per-run refit

Baseline: `47f090229bc6f02c6273ae5e6ce6e6d66c6b5c26`, with the part-(d) result in `g6_couple/response.json`. From the repository root, `python -m g6_plateau.forecast` writes `forecasts.csv` and `discriminators.csv`. It does not access Zenodo, fit a new bulk screening shift, or choose a cause from the digitized curves.

## Forward forecasts

The first three candidates use the best rows of `g4_beam/merit/fits_transported.json`; the pass-4 uniform fits in `g4_beam/forward/fits.json` stay separate. The uniform high-energy PdD fit (1.40235 keV) supplies the unchanged background for the lattice and resonance-control rows. The source chi² column describes the prior fit, not a new score for the fourth cause. Every PdD comparison retains the transported volumetric profile, outer D sheet, beam fractions and spread. The target-side quadrature is the same stopping solution as `forward.consumer`.

`forecasts.csv` compares **total fusion events per true incident coulomb** at matched charged-beam **laboratory** energies, not counts in dissimilar detectors. For the PdT counterfactual, freeze the PdD fuel *number* profile and the fitted shifts, substitute tritium in that profile and sheet, use the D–T center-of-mass energy, recoil mass and the Bosch–Hale D–T cross section. Do not carry the D–D threshold term into D–T. The D–T coefficients are those of [Bosch & Hale](https://doi.org/10.1088/0029-5515/32/4/I07), cross-checked against the [LLNL implementation](https://github.com/llnl/pyPICfusion/blob/master/_BoschHale_library.py); D–T behavior at the lowest energies is a parametrization extrapolation rather than a measured transfer of the plateau.

LiD is a bulk rocksalt replacement with one D per Li (`a=4.0648 Å`, twice the observed ion spacing in [Pandey & Stoneham](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3719/18/27/015)), no unmeasured adsorbate and the same Lindhard–Scharff/ZBL prescription. Both fixed-PdD-shift transfer and zero-LiD-shift scenarios are shown: neither is a LiD fit, and the pair of scenarios is not a material uncertainty interval. The two ratios can differ by many orders below the knee. The fitted neutral source still strikes the LiD column in the zero-shift case. No LiD locked state or state-dependent electronic response is supplied; the lattice row gives the base beam counterfactual, not a prediction of that unknown term.

At 0.5 keV lab the D–T/D–D ratios for screening rise, the canonical surface fit, neutrals and the lattice background are 2.60, 3.18, 55.6 and 2.54 respectively. The different kinematics make a neutral-dominated floor materially different under isotope substitution. The common D–D resonance multiplies *total* D–D yield by 5.02 at this energy in the unchanged bulk control, by 1.23 at 2 keV, and by 0.979 at 13 keV; interference can reduce the yield. It is a shared nuclear sensitivity, not a fifth candidate or an inferred bound-pair width.

## Pair at rest: what the four causes actually predict

| Cause | Added rate per pair per second in a bulk pair with the beam off |
|---|---|
| Screening rise | Not identified by an incident-energy sigmoid. A nonzero change requires a static local potential and a normalized bound state; substituting the fitted `Ues` for these is not justified. |
| Surface layer | Zero incremental bulk term in this model. A pair in the layer is a different state; its contact rate is not fixed by the fitted layer thickness or beam shift. |
| Fast neutrals | Zero incremental term when the neutral flux is zero. This does not set the underlying bound-pair rate. |
| Lattice state | The frozen PdD shift lowers the BO screening reduction by 0.687 eV at 0.1 Å. The seven-point interior curve identifies neither an origin continuation nor a prepared well, so no material contact rate follows. Fixed relative potential under a pure common translation is a separate zero-change control. |
| Common D–D resonance | No finite bound rate follows from the on-shell `reaction_S(E)` fit with a positive-energy threshold term. A matched bound-channel width/matrix element is needed; do not evaluate its divergent algebraic continuation at zero. |

## Discriminators and the primary-data refit

`discriminators.csv` joins the original three pass-5 interventions to the reduced lattice model. Its lattice **total** response includes ordinary density and beam-switch effects. The incremental fixed-pole ionic locked/unlocked contrast at a fixed intervention is exactly one. The frozen-spectator envelope remains conditional, and neither ionic diagnostic should be added on top of the matched electronic calculation. A material state effect should track loading, temperature, drive and phase correlation after ordinary transport and beam distributions are accounted for. A temperature scan alone does not distinguish it from adsorption or transport.

The lattice hypothesis in `forecasts.csv` now carries a reference to the [seven-radius collision contrast](../g6_couple/collision_projection.json) and separate BO and anchored encounter-work endpoint columns. At 0.1 Å, the imposed 0.0768 Å [100] displacement changes BO lowering by **−0.687 eV** and work supplied to the system by **+0.675 eV** in the 2 keV head-on case. This shows no resolved enhancement. The additional `hypothetical_rigid_shift` column is a **D(d,p)T yield/C sensitivity**, not a prediction of the measured material response: it tests −0.675 eV uniformly added to a 40 eV Eq. 7 reference with a fixed transported profile. It is not applied to the 1.402 keV fitted background or to the isotope switch; other energies and geometry are not constrained by the path. No qualified beam amplitude or prepared-pair rate follows from the radial support. The original forecasts and intervention ratios are unchanged.

The `target_tritium_fraction=1` discriminator is the same-lab-energy D–T/D–D counterfactual at 0.5 keV lab. Its `E_cm_keV` is the nominal D–D axis; D–T uses its own center-of-mass conversion. The lattice number is a fixed-background isotope ratio, not a measured change in locking or electronic response. Empty lattice-state fields on this row mean those quantities were not identified for the isotope swap.

`g4_zenodo` owns ingest after release, the common run mask and the per-run refit. Join each hypothesis to the same `run_id`, actual foil/history, incident species/energy, true delivered charge, and calibrated detector channel. See `g4_beam/causes/zenodo_columns.csv`. Compare which interventions are supported by the published metadata; do not assign nominal substitutes to missing true charge, density, neutral flux or state. Keep the same bulk screening table while refitting plateau quantities. If event counts are sparse or the background-subtracted signal is nonpositive, use the raw likelihood with background/exposure; no log transform of censored or invented counts. A calibrated proton peak can distinguish a shifted center of mass from a real pair at rest independently of the yield comparison.

For a calibrated positive-count join, `python -m g6_plateau.score_runs runs.csv log_covariance.npz` profiles shared multiplicative normalization and returns chi²/dof for each of the four supplied predictions. CSV columns: `run_id` (unique scored observation, include detector suffix if needed), `normalization_group` (`absolute` or a named shared nuisance group), `observed_yield_per_true_C`, `screeningrise`, `surface`, `neutrals`, `lattice` (same per-C units and same detector/channel convention). Supply NPZ arrays `run_id` (Unicode strings) and `covariance` (symmetric positive-definite covariance of natural-log yields with that axis ordering); the CLI joins by ID, not row order. Feed predictions at the measured runs from the receiving fit, not from the two sample curves by guessing unobserved fields. A shape tie is a tie; the frozen fourth cause cannot fit a separate amplitude without a measured electronic/state input. No actual per-run score is claimed before release.

Three dominant omissions: the per-run charge/reference covariance and actual intervention fields; isotope- and LiD-specific electronic screening, surface chemistry and stopping; and the microscopic bound-pair/electronic-response input to an identified lattice state.
