# PdD force constants for the atomistic block

Compute the full coupled Pd/D harmonic force-constant matrix of stoichiometric rocksalt PdD at the existing fixed 4.084 Å cell, using the native finite-electron-temperature PBE Helmholtz free-energy forces. Feed G17 the real-space matrix and its temperature difference from 300 K, not a fitted shell law or a list of selected wave vectors. Set both ionic species free in the dynamical matrix. Do not synthesize a thermal linewidth from static forces.

## Inputs and numerical comparison

Use the same Pd.10/H PAW sets, PW 500 eV, neutral spin-paired state and Fermi occupations as the archived branch. Both sublattices are internally stationary by the rocksalt site symmetry while the common fixed volume allows the Te comparison. Use the accepted thermal ladder at 300, 310, 350, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000 and 30,000 K, plus the 1,500 and 2,000 K crossover cases. Order: 300, 3,000, 10,000 and 30,000 K anchor the response first; then 1,000, 1,500, 2,000, 310 and 350 K. For each compare exact folds of the parent 12³ and 16³ electronic meshes, and preserve the PAW and occupation-tail receipts. The old folded Γ/X results are independent clamped-Pd block comparisons at 300/3,000/10,000 K; they are not the frequencies of mixed Pd/D optical modes.

Start with a 2×2×2 repeat of the **primitive** cell (16 atoms) and obtain both inequivalent full force response columns from 0.01 Å Phonopy displacements. Each atom’s cubic site symmetry generates the other Cartesian directions; derive the negative displacement by inversion of the force vector and atom permutation about the displaced site when the electronic grid and undeflected geometry retain this operation. Compare independent negative displacements on the reference lane. Record undistorted residual forces, native sign-transfer residual, force constants before and after acoustic and reciprocity projection, signed phonon frequencies, and both k-mesh results. If the precision of a heating change is insufficient, report its actual mesh movement instead of treating near-zero results as settled. A larger 2×2×2 **conventional** cell (64 atoms) makes X/2 commensurate along each cubic axis, and can resolve finite-supercell effects on the interpolated full-zone frequencies. The 16-atom cell contains Γ and X but not X/2; label intermediate q values as interpolation from its retained force constants.

At the same parent 16³ electronic mesh, compare the 16- and 64-atom cells at 300 and 3,000 K before using either thermal tensor in MD. Use the same signed 0.01 Å Cartesian [011] displacement in both cells. Compare the same six-dimensional reciprocal stiffness in the common Pd/D Cartesian basis, not real-space array elements with different alias multiplicities. Record both absolute spectra, the signed modes, and the error in the hot-minus-cold matrix at every q. Size screen: operator norm of the difference ≤ max(0.002 eV/Å², 10% of the larger-cell thermal matrix norm) at each q. If it does not agree, retain the 64-atom result as a size sensitivity, not a converged tensor; decide using the observed effect whether to resolve it in the next larger commensurate cell. The size screen and electronic mesh check are distinct.

The 16→64 comparison failed. The next size is a 4×4×4 repeat of the primitive cell (128 atoms): its translations are contained in the 64-atom cell’s translation lattice, so its reciprocal grid includes the smaller cell’s exact q points. Use the same [011] step and parent 16³ mesh, initially at 300 and 3,000 K. At this size the original empty-band provision is memory-limited. Test seven bands per PdD formula unit in a separate 16-atom 3,000 K k16 run against the retained ten-band forces: require both native occupation tails ≤10⁻¹⁰ and a maximum change in the unmassweighted reciprocal stiffness norm of ≤0.0005 eV/Å² over the same full q grid. Require the tighter tail for each 128-atom native point too. The band-count control and one retained 128-atom D displacement decide local admission; stop on any unqualified result. Compare 64→128 with the original size screen, and check the electronic mesh separately before MD use. Different temperature ranges, defects and loadings remain outside this check.

The first band control passed the occupation checks but failed the operator check: the maximum change was 0.002130 eV/Å². The force difference is predominantly displacement-odd (up to 3.85×10⁻⁶ eV/Å) and comparable to the previous force stopping threshold. This does not identify which force vector is closer to the converged result. Compare both band counts at the same tighter SCF settings (force change 2×10⁻⁸ eV/Å and density 2×10⁻⁸) before changing either the cutoff or the occupation tolerance. Report the shift of the tighter ten-band reference from its own original result. If the matched tighter profiles still disagree by more than 0.0005 eV/Å² or fail native qualification, stop the compact path rather than assuming they are equivalent. If they pass, use the tighter settings for the 128-atom check as well.

## Replay and use

Phonopy assembles the full force constants and produces an FCC band path plus all six branches and eigenvectors on a Γ-centered 24³ primitive reciprocal mesh. Keep all imaginary modes as signed frequencies. Compare clamped-D blocks at Γ/X against the old folded responses (target max 0.002 eV/Å² or 1% of the old value, whichever is greater) separately from the coupled eigenfrequencies. Record per-polarization changes and their k-mesh spread; retain native full arrays and use Phonopy and GPAW/ASE for replay. Never assign a sign to a heating shift merely by sorting eigenvectors at a crossing: keep dynamical matrices or force constants to permit subspace comparison.

The three largest omissions are finite-supercell periodic aliasing of longer-ranged forces, fixed-volume fully loaded bulk rather than the loading/defect/surface distribution in the block, and harmonic adiabatic response at a spatially uniform electronic temperature rather than dissipative electronic dynamics. For two-temperature MD, supply the temperature-dependent harmonic **difference** as a material reference; replacing or adding a total baseline force without removing the potential’s own cold contribution would double count it.
