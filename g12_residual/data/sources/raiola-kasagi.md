## Bochum / LUNA: early and expanded surveys

- **Raiola et al., Phys. Lett. B 547 (2002) 193–199**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0370-2693(02)02774-0). The XML does not embed the figure image. O1381002–5 are Au, Al, Pb and Pd. The archive field says `EN`, but Fig. 1 explicitly says **effective center-of-mass energy**. The unsupported `DATA-ERR` cells remain blank; the CAJAD digitization allowances are not substitutes for point errors.
- In that paper the 5–30-keV incident-energy **thick** yields were differenced per energy step; energy-dependent stopping, effective energy, and cross-section conversion yield the plotted **S(E)**, not a thick yield or a direct ratio. The absolute scale was normalized at *incident* `Ed=30 keV` to Greife et al., including screening where applicable. The fit used bare `S_b(E)=43+0.54 E` in keV·b with `E` in c.m. keV (§2, Eq. 2 analysis). The printed stoichiometry is **hostₓD**. Where elemental, `actual_D_per_metal` is `1/x`, an inferred near-surface loading, not an assay (author estimate ∼20% relative error). ∼1 C to saturation is typical, not a per-run measurement. ERDA found approximately uniform D within ∼10% over the stopping range in most cases, not universal. No numerical points were taken from the binary shading in Fig. 2. The Al points are labeled Al in the 2002 paper, but the author later attributed the unsputtered low result to the native Al₂O₃ surface (thesis §3.1, Fig. 3.3); this was not pooled with cleaned Al.

## Tohoku: plain Pd versus oxide-facing layered foils

- **Yuki, Kasagi et al., JETP Lett. 68 (1998) 823–829**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1134/1.567800), [English journal PDF archive](https://lenr-canr.org/acrobat/YukiHanomalouse.pdf). Table I p. 826 gives `U_s = 250.1±16.0` eV at 190.1 K and `257.5±21.6` eV at 313.0 K for plain Pd; `601.7±23.4` eV at 193.3 K for Au/Pd/PdO. Its D/Me values (0.23±0.10, 0.38±0.12, 0.11±0.07 respectively) are inferred means at `Ed=10 keV` (p. 825, Eq. 2); D loading and temperature co-vary, so this is not an isolated temperature intervention. At p. 824, plain Pd/Ti/Au were 100 µm foils vacuum-annealed 5–10 h at 800 °C; the multilayer was 60 µm, ∼40 nm PdO and ∼0.1 µm Au on the opposite side. The 60–240 µA scan is a range, not a point log. At pp. 824–25 ERDA D extent is 0–100 nm in Pd and >250 nm in Au/Pd/PdO; the estimated incident-ion stopping range for **Pd only** is 20–40 nm across 5–10 keV. These are different quantities. Fig. 1 p. 826 and Fig. 2 p. 827 give thick proton yields on the 10-keV anchored scale, with `Ed` in the **laboratory**. Fig. 3 p. 828 divides by the calculated same-energy bare thick yield (Bosch–Hale cross section, low-E velocity-proportional stopping, anchored at 10 keV), not by a different specimen. Only the ∼50× layered-target point at 2.5 keV is kept from Fig. 3; it shares its measured numerator with the Fig. 2 marker. Raster reading omitted obscured centers/caps. Reference unity at 10 keV is tagged as such, not measured again.
- **Kasagi et al., J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 71 (2002) 2881–2885**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1143/JPSJ.71.2881), [published-layout PDF archive](https://www.lenr-canr.org/acrobat/KasagiJstronglyen.pdf). Fe Ti Au etc remain in their published labels.
